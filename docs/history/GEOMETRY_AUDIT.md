## Audited boundaries and known limits

* Fit probes are not final toolpath certification. Orca's arranged project retains the active printer's native exclusion polygon. The independent arranged-envelope check conservatively refuses overlap, including feature reserve. It can refuse a concave part or separated copies whose combined bounding rectangle crosses an exclusion even when no triangle does. The final slicer/toolpath safety gates remain mandatory. Multi-plate fit uses rectangular envelopes and two cardinal yaws only; it is not a continuous placement solver.
* `export_color_geometry` cannot safely flatten a reflected painted mesh: paint subdivision trees depend on vertex ordering. It refuses that case before writing a result. Two reflections compose to an orientation-preserving transform and remain supported. No unverified paint-tree permutation is attempted. Follow-up audit (#1195): the volume-plate export and the per-part export also refuse a reflected leaf that carries support, seam or fuzzy-skin paint (`paint_supports`, `paint_seam`, PrusaSlicer `custom_supports` and `custom_seam`), which use the same vertex-order encoding and which those exports keep; rewinding would have moved painted enforcers or seams to sub-triangles nobody painted. The per-color source reader (`painted_geometry.paint_census`, `source_colors.inventory`) counts states per triangle and never flattens geometry, so a reflected source reads exactly like an unmirrored one (`tests/core/test_mirrored_transforms.py`). The in-place paint remap keeps the source's own transforms. The support-clearance reader and the triangle flatteners rewind reflected leaves and carry no paint.
* Split-piece volume sums are **not comprehensively audited** by this follow-up. Existing split/topology tests do not prove conservation for every connector, clearance or Boolean operation. Material removed by kerfs, bores and sockets or added by joinery makes a blanket sum-equals-original assertion invalid. No new conservation guarantee is claimed. Follow-up audit (#1195): the CAD split already checks each half watertight, one shell, inside the plan's bed, and removed volume against the bore volume. The AI-fix split (`ai_fix.split_model`) assigns whole triangles and builds no cap, so a plane through connected surface left both parts as open shells with no defined volume (a 30 mm bar split in half measured 1000 and 2000 mm3). That split is now refused; a split that separates pieces sharing no edge keeps closed parts whose volumes sum to the source's (`tests/test_ai_fix_985.py`). Since #1245 the AI-fix split is a real capped plane cut (`core/plane_cut.py`): crossing triangles are clipped, the outline is capped with holes, and each part is checked closed with the two volumes summing to the source's before anything is written; the 30 mm bar now splits into two closed 1500 mm3 halves. Split plates from the full-size planner are whole objects, checked against the printer's XY and Z limits by `split_fit.per_part_fit`.
* Assembly collision coverage is in `tests/core/test_assembly_declarations.py`: flush contact, separated parts, crossing slabs, identical placements, aligned overlaps, partial placement, and the 0.01 mm contact-band unknown result. The implementation is a floating-point witness search, not a general exact solid-intersection proof. Degenerate/nonmanifold input, very thin coplanar overlaps and subresolution overlap remain limits, not certified clearance. No collision gate has been relaxed by this work. Follow-up audit (#1195) adds concave and contained cases: a peg through a tube's bore is judged by material, not bounds (0.15 mm clearance passes; 0.3 and 0.05 mm interference collide, with the peg's facets aligned or turned), a part wholly inside another collides, and a rotated placement is checked through its turned geometry.
* Lay-flat now ranks actual supporting-plane area for each sampled face-normal direction. Normals are grouped at two decimal places and contact uses 0.01 mm tolerance. This is not a continuous stability solver or a guarantee of adhesion; thin feet, curved surfaces and geometry below the tolerance remain limitations. Support checks still apply to the resulting orientation.
* The wedge-root defect has a synthetic regression. Follow-up (#1195): `tests/corpus/test_wedge_root_real_models.py` pins the rule on four real corpus-v1 models with bottom chamfers (run by the model-corpus workflow on the verified download), including three whose chamfer runs round the whole base and is excused only through the thin-strip proof. Classifying all 171 corpus-v1 models with the thin-strip proof disabled (the loosened pre-#1137 rule) changed no model's result, so no real model in the corpus reproduces the wide-faceted-underside defect; the synthetic sawtooth stays its regression.
