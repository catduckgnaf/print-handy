# Telling defects from design

Defect: geometry the designer did not intend and nobody would miss.

- Sub-millimeter sag below a flat face, for example a deck whose underside
  dips up to 0.72 mm under its own plane. Trimming it back to the plane
  removes a support-hungry sliver and changes nothing visible.
- Tiny undercuts at the base of a part, a fraction of a millimeter, that the
  first layer cannot reach. A straight-walled base removes them.
- Stray bodies of a few faces, open edges and inverted faces.

Design: geometry that is there on purpose.

- A shelf, ledge or lip, for example a 26 mm deep cantilever. It cannot be
  trimmed away. It needs a 45 degree gusset or fill under it, supports, or a
  different orientation, and the owner chooses which.
- A long flat ceiling anchored at both ends, which may bridge (see bridges).

When unsure, measure: size, height above what is below, and slope. A
feature larger than a few millimeters is almost always design.
